STEINS;OS — V2 Design & Engineering Review
From: Burrito 🌯 — Design Reviewer
To: Alex Ultra / Codexito — Lead Engineer
Status: Review complete; refinement and validation requested
Project: Steins;OS / Project Worldline

1. Overall assessment

Alex,

The latest preview demonstrates substantial progress towards the Steins;OS vision. The Akihabara sunset worldline, Hinamizawa artwork, desktop shell, divergence indicators, Worldline navigator, terminal, notification, power dialog, lock screen and transition storyboard share an increasingly coherent aesthetic.

The anime illustrations are especially successful. They establish a beautiful environment without requiring the interface itself to be overloaded with anime imagery.

The next milestone is not simply additional features. It is to make the system feel like one original, recognisable operating system, rather than a collection of independently styled components.

Preserve the successful artwork and existing functionality. Prioritise the refinements below.

2. Priority 1 — Everyday terminal identity

The terminal is functional-looking, with tabs, shell output and a divergence display. However, visually it still resembles a conventional terminal with a custom colour scheme.

Please refine it into a recognisable Steins;OS flagship application.

Explore:

- A distinctive title bar and tab design consistent with the shell.
- An integrated but restrained divergence indicator.
- Worldline-aware accent colours and status treatments.
- Carefully designed typography, spacing, cursor and selection colours.
- Optional compact status information that does not compete with terminal output.
- Consistent visual behaviour in maximised, tiled and floating states.

Keep it genuinely practical: Bash/Zsh, real PTY behaviour, terminal escape sequences, copy/paste, scrolling, keyboard shortcuts, resizing, SSH, Vim/Neovim, Unicode and long-running processes must remain reliable.

Do not sacrifice terminal usability for decorative effects.

Maintain the distinction between the modern everyday terminal and the retro IBN 5100-inspired Worldline terminal. Both belong to the same OS but serve different purposes.

Acceptance: The everyday terminal should look unmistakably Steins;OS even when viewed without the wallpaper.

3. Priority 2 — Boot and login experience

The previewed Steins;OS loading screen feels too static for the intended identity.

Develop a more dynamic, cinematic but restrained startup sequence.

Potential direction:

1. Dark background with a subtle signal or scanline emergence.
2. Steins;OS symbol assembling from light traces or geometric fragments.
3. Divergence digits briefly appearing, rolling or stabilising.
4. A short original system-identification cue.
5. A clean transition into the login screen or desktop.

Keep the experience fast and appropriate for repeated daily use.

Support reduced motion and silence, and avoid excessive flashes or long unskippable sequences.

Crucially, determine whether this is a design preview, a login animation, a Plymouth boot splash, or an actual implemented end-to-end sequence. Integrate only through appropriate Linux boot/display-manager mechanisms, without compromising reliable startup.

4. Priority 3 — Logo review

The current circular, gold-toned emblem is an interesting starting point, but I am not yet convinced it is the right long-term symbol.

It feels somewhat generic compared with the uniqueness of the Worldline concept.

Please explore a small set of original alternatives, such as:

- A minimal geometric worldline/divergence mark.
- An abstract temporal intersection or branching symbol.
- A distinctive Steins;OS monogram with subtle scientific instrumentation influences.

The mark must work in monochrome, at tiny panel-icon sizes, on a login screen and as a larger boot emblem.

Avoid direct copying of Steins;Gate franchise logos or assets.

Do not replace the existing logo automatically. Present two or three candidates alongside the current mark, with rationale and practical mock-ups, for approval.

5. Priority 4 — Worldline shift: the signature experience

The approximately 800 ms five-stage storyboard is a promising design:

Activate → Instability → Distort → Transform → Stabilise

Now establish how much of this is actually implemented.

The transition should feel like a brief, coordinated reality shift rather than a simple wallpaper replacement.

Synchronise, where technically feasible:

- Divergence digit movement.
- Subtle visual distortion or chromatic separation.
- Worldline wallpaper change.
- Shell accent and terminal palette changes.
- Native panel and navigator state.
- A short optional original sound cue.
- A clean stabilisation without lost application state.

All entry points — navigator, divergence meter, IBN terminal and any future shortcuts — should use one authoritative Worldline state service.

Ensure reduced-motion and instant-change fallbacks. Never interrupt active applications or leave mismatched colours after a failed transition.

Acceptance: A shift changes the appropriate system elements coherently, and the user can continue working immediately.

6. Priority 5 — Audio integration audit

We cannot confirm from the screenshots whether the sound system was integrated after the latest changes.

Please inspect the actual implementation and report what exists.

Consider original, subtle cues for:

- Successful Worldline shift.
- Shift initiation or stabilisation.
- Login readiness.
- Notifications.
- Error or rejected command.

Audio should be optional, volume-controlled and respectful of mute settings.

Do not use copyrighted anime or game sound samples without appropriate permission.

Do not add sounds everywhere. Silence is part of a good interface.

7. Priority 6 — System-wide visual consistency

Review the entire prototype with the wallpaper hidden or replaced by a neutral background.

Inspect:

- Application launcher.
- Panel and task switching.
- Window decorations.
- Notifications.
- Power confirmation.
- Settings and accessibility.
- Worldline navigator.
- Divergence meter.
- Login and lock screen.
- Terminal and file manager.

Identify inconsistent spacing, typography, border radii, accent colours, icon styles, window controls and interaction patterns.

The shell should have a clear visual grammar, not simply matching dark blue surfaces.

Pay particular attention to the bottom panel. It is improving, but should become more distinctively Steins;OS.

Do not rebuild reliable Linux infrastructure unnecessarily. Prefer maintainable integrations with the existing desktop/compositor where practical.

8. Worldline artwork

Preserve the illustrated Akihabara and Hinamizawa directions shown in the previews.

They are among the strongest parts of the current design.

Continue with original anime-style environments for the other worldlines, while preserving a coherent level of artistic quality.

Worldlines should feel like different places and atmospheres, not merely colour-filtered versions of one wallpaper.

Avoid photorealistic replacements.

9. VM test environment

Please assess whether we can build a repeatable, isolated VM test environment for the current Steins;OS prototype.

Preferred outcome:

- Bootable test image or reproducible installation process.
- QEMU/KVM where supported.
- Plasma/Wayland compatibility assessment.
- Appropriate virtual GPU configuration.
- Snapshots or rollback before changes.
- Clearly documented launch and reset commands.
- No destructive changes to the host system.

Test startup, login, terminal, shell interactions, Worldline switching, notifications, audio, lock screen and shutdown.

If graphical acceleration, Wayland effects or sound are limited in the VM, document those limitations and distinguish them from actual product defects.

Do not overwrite or migrate the existing working environment without approval.

10. Engineer-led polish pass

Beyond the issues identified above, use your own engineering and design judgement to identify the highest-impact refinements.

Look for genuine usability, stability, accessibility, performance and consistency problems.

Fix low-risk defects within the agreed scope. For major architectural changes, destructive operations or new dependencies, explain the trade-offs and request approval first.

Avoid feature creep. Prioritise making existing components feel complete.

11. Deliverables requested

Please return:

1. Implementation inventory: Working components, partial implementations, mock-ups and unimplemented concepts.
2. Review response: Which recommendations you agree with, which you would modify, and why.
3. Polish plan: Prioritised tasks with dependencies and expected outcomes.
4. Logo concepts: Current design versus two or three alternatives, without replacing the approved design.
5. Terminal refinement: Updated preview plus technical explanation of the terminal architecture.
6. Boot/login concept: Improved animation preview and integration feasibility.
7. Audio audit: Confirmed implementation status and missing integration points.
8. VM proposal or working test environment: With documented limitations and reproducible setup.
9. Verification evidence: Commands, test results, screenshots or recordings where appropriate.

12. Review standard

Steins;OS should ultimately feel like a distinct anime-inspired Linux operating system, not a conventional desktop with anime wallpapers.

It must also remain a reliable computer for real everyday work.

We are not chasing visual effects for their own sake. We are building a coherent interface whose most memorable moment is shifting between worldlines.

Preserve the working prototype, show the evidence, refine deliberately, and let the Chairman approve major design decisions.
